## Jurisdiction decisions

Intended starting audience remains US English in a **counsel-approved subset**; no actual subset or merchant entity has been supplied. Do not substitute US country geolocation for a state-law/content review. All unapproved/uncertain regions receive the same closed restricted-content state.

- **US:** rules differ by state and actual content classification. [Texas Chapter 129B](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CP/htm/CP.129B.htm) applies specified age duties to covered commercial websites exceeding the statutory sexual-material threshold; definitions include descriptions, so text-based games are not automatically exempt. The [Supreme Court's Paxton decision](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1122_3e04.pdf) upheld the challenged requirement under intermediate scrutiny. Neither source clears every state, every activity or every statutory provision. Counsel must produce a dated state-by-state launch/exclusion matrix, including retention restrictions, enforcement and pending injunctions.
- **UK:** [Ofcom guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-assurance) requires highly effective age assurance for in-scope pornography services. Self-declaration and ordinary contractual warnings do not qualify. Methods may include ID, facial estimation, digital identity or bank/network approaches, subject to effectiveness criteria. Account email OTP is inbox ownership, not Ofcom's email-based age estimation. UK restricted content stays closed pending classification, provider adequacy and UK privacy review.
- **France/EU:** [CNIL's review of the Arcom standard](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/verification-de-lage-en-ligne-la-cnil-rend-son-avis-sur-le-referentiel-de-larcom) describes independent providers and privacy requirements. Counsel must review current Arcom requirements, including double anonymity and method choice, rather than treating an ID upload as sufficient. The [EU age-verification solution](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/eu-age-verification-solution) is designed around over-age proofs without sharing name or birth date; country readiness and acceptance must still be verified. It is not a single commercial provider or EU-wide launch permission. GDPR special-category implications, DPIA, lawful basis, transfers and national rules remain gates.
- **Other countries:** unsupported for restricted content until separately reviewed. IP location is a routing signal with VPN/mislocation limits, not proof of age or residence. This milestone does not collect location claims to manufacture clearance.

## Replaceable provider boundary

`AgeAssuranceProvider` defines begin/verifyCallback/revoke, with a hosted redirect and minimal over18 assertion. No implementation, webhook, document upload or client-issued assertion is accepted. CheapSeggs must never receive/store ID images, selfies, raw DOB, legal name, full provider responses or biometric templates.

A future integration must use the maintained vendor SDK or documented signing mechanism; validate signature/key rotation, audience, subject, nonce, access epoch, market policy, issue/expiry times and unique assertion ID. Persist a one-time challenge/consumption record and minimal assertion only after validation; implement provider revocation/expiry and account-deletion propagation. Expiring/pending/failed assertions grant nothing. Withdrawal invalidates outstanding challenges by epoch. Reverification cannot silently re-enable categories. Add callback replay, wrong-account, jurisdiction, failure/expiry and race tests before opening the adapter. The existing generic `adult_access` table alone is insufficient evidence of these controls.
